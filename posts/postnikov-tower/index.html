If $X$ and $Y$ are topological spaces, a map $f: X \to Y$ is said to be an $n$-equivalence if it induces a bijection $\pi_0(X) \to \pi_0(Y)$ (the sets of path-connected components of $X$ and $Y$ respectively) and for all choice of a base-point $x \in X$, an isomorphism of groups $\pi_i(X,x) \to \pi_i(Y,f(x))$ for all $i < n$ and a surjection for $i=n$. Moreover, $f$ is a weak homotopy equivalence if it is an $n$-equivalence for all $n$.

A map $p: E \to B$ is a (Serre) fibration if it has the right lifting property with respect to the maps $D^n \times \{0\} \to D^n \times I$.

If $f: X \to Y$ is any map, there is a functorial way to factorize it as $X \to P_f \to Y$ where $X \to P_f$ is a homotopy equivalence and $P_f \to Y$ is a (Serre) fibration. Then we define the homotopy fibre $F_f(y)$ of a map $f: X \to Y$ and a point $y \in Y$ as the fibre of $P_f \to Y$ over $y$. This is exactly the homotopy pullback, computed as the ordinary pullback after a fibrant remplacement.

If $p: E \to B$ is a Serre fibration, $e \in E$ is a point, $F=p^{-1}(p(e))$ is the fibre of $p(e)$, then the sequence

$$ (F,e) \to (E,e) \to (B,p(e)) $$

gives rise to a long exact sequence

$$ \ldots \pi_n(F,e) \to \pi_n(E,e) \to \pi_n(B,p(e)) \to \pi_{n-1}(F,e) \to \ldots \to \pi_0(F,e) \to \pi_0(E,e) \to \pi_0(B,p(e)). $$

Postnikov Towers

Let $X$ be a connected topological space. The Postnikov tower of $X$ is a sequence of spaces and maps

$$ \cdots \to P_n X \to P_{n-1} X \to \cdots \to P_1 X \to P_0 X $$

together with maps $X \to P_n X$ satisfying:

  1. $X \to P_n X$ is an $n$-equivalence.
  2. $\pi_i(P_n X) = 0$ for all $i > n$.

Each $P_n X$ is called the $n$-th Postnikov stage of $X$.

Construction Sketch

  1. Start with $P_0 X = K(\pi_0(X),0)$.
  2. Inductively, given $P_{n-1} X$, define $P_n X$ by killing homotopy in degree $> n$ using fibrations and homotopy fibres.
  3. Each step involves a fibration sequence $$ K(\pi_n(X),n) \to P_n X \to P_{n-1} X. $$

Here $K(\pi_n(X),n)$ is the Eilenberg–MacLane space of type $(\pi_n(X),n)$.

Properties of Postnikov Towers

  1. The tower comes equipped with maps $X \to P_n X$ which are $n$-equivalences.
  2. The homotopy groups of $P_n X$ are $$ \pi_i(P_n X) \cong \begin{cases} \pi_i(X) & i \le n \\ 0 & i > n. \end{cases} $$
  3. The tower is functorial in $X$.
  4. The inverse limit of the tower recovers $X$ up to weak homotopy equivalence: $$ X \simeq \varprojlim P_n X. $$
