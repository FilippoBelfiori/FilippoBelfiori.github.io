Chern Classes in Ordinary Cohomology

Let $X$ be a topological space and let $E \to X$ be a complex vector bundle of rank $n$. The Chern classes of $E$ are characteristic classes

$$ c_i(E) \in H^{2i}(X;\mathbb{Z}), \qquad i=0,\dots,n, $$

with $c_0(E)=1$, which measure the twisting of the bundle $E$.

The total Chern class is defined as

$$ c(E) = 1 + c_1(E) + c_2(E) + \cdots + c_n(E) \in H^{\ast}(X;\mathbb{Z}). $$

Classifying Space Construction

For every $n$, there exists a classifying space $BU(n)$ and a universal complex vector bundle

$$ \gamma_n \to BU(n). $$

The integral cohomology ring of $BU(n)$ is a polynomial ring

$$ H^\ast(BU(n);\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}[c_1,\dots,c_n], $$

where $c_i$ are the universal Chern classes.

If a rank $n$ bundle $E \to X$ is classified by a map

$$ f : X \to BU(n), $$

then the Chern classes of $E$ are defined by pullback:

$$ c_i(E) := f^\ast(c_i). $$

Basic Properties

Let $E,F$ be complex vector bundles over $X$.

  1. (Naturality) If $g : Y \to X$, then

    $$ c_i(g^\ast E) = g^\ast c_i(E). $$

  2. (Whitney Sum Formula)

    $$ c(E \oplus F) = c(E) \smile c(F). $$

  3. (Normalization) For the trivial bundle $\varepsilon^n$,

    $$ c_i(\varepsilon^n)=0 \quad \text{for } i>0. $$

  4. (Dimension) If $\operatorname{rank}(E)=n$, then

    $$ c_i(E)=0 \quad \text{for } i>n. $$

Line Bundles

If $L \to X$ is a complex line bundle, then

$$ c(L) = 1 + c_1(L), $$

and $c_i(L)=0$ for $i>1$.

Moreover, isomorphism classes of complex line bundles over $X$ correspond to elements of $H^2(X;\mathbb{Z})$ via the first Chern class:

$$ L \longmapsto c_1(L). $$

Splitting Principle

There exists a space $\tilde X \to X$ such that:

  • The induced map in cohomology is injective.
  • The pulled-back bundle splits as a sum of line bundles:
$$ \pi^\ast E \cong L_1 \oplus \cdots \oplus L_n. $$

If we define formal Chern roots

$$ x_i := c_1(L_i), $$

then

$$ c(E) = \prod_{i=1}^n (1 + x_i). $$

All polynomial identities between Chern classes follow from symmetric polynomial identities in the $x_i$.

Top Chern Class and Euler Class

If $\operatorname{rank}(E)=n$, then the top Chern class

$$ c_n(E) \in H^{2n}(X;\mathbb{Z}) $$

coincides with the Euler class of the underlying oriented real bundle of rank $2n$.

Chern Character

There is a natural transformation from complex $K$-theory to rational cohomology called the Chern character:

$$ \operatorname{ch} : K^0(X) \to H^{\ast}(X;\mathbb{Q}), $$

defined in terms of Chern classes by

$$ \operatorname{ch}(E) = \sum_{i=1}^n e^{x_i}, $$

where $x_i$ are the Chern roots (via the splitting principle).
