1. The Two Main Objects

The paper constructs and studies two categories:

  • $\mathrm{Sp}^\Sigma$ — symmetric spectra of simplicial sets (Sections 1–5)
  • $\mathrm{Sp}^\Sigma_{\mathcal{T}_*}$ — symmetric spectra of compactly generated topological spaces (Section 6)

The simplicial case is the primary one. Section 6 then lifts the whole theory to the topological setting via geometric realization / singular functor, and the two stable model categories turn out to be Quillen equivalent.

2. What Is a Symmetric Spectrum?

Ordinary spectrum (Definition 1.2.1)

A sequence of pointed simplicial sets $X_0, X_1, \ldots$ together with structure maps $\sigma: S^1 \wedge X_n \to X_{n+1}$.

Symmetric spectrum (Definition 1.2.2)

Same data plus: a basepoint-preserving left action of $\Sigma_n$ on $X_n$ for each $n$, such that the iterated structure map

$$\sigma^p = \sigma \circ (S^1 \wedge \sigma) \circ \cdots \circ (S^{p-1} \wedge \sigma) : S^p \wedge X_n \to X_{n+p}$$

is $\Sigma_p \times \Sigma_n$-equivariant for all $p \geq 1$, $n \geq 0$.

Key slogan: $S^p = (S^1)^{\wedge p}$ carries a left permutation action of $\Sigma_p$; the symmetric spectrum must be compatible with this action at every level.

Note (Remark 1.2.3): It suffices to require equivariance for $\sigma$ and $\sigma^2$ alone (since $\Sigma_p$ is generated by transpositions).

The category of symmetric spectra is denoted $\mathrm{Sp}^\Sigma$; maps are sequences of $\Sigma_n$-equivariant pointed maps commuting with structure maps.

3. The Closed Symmetric Monoidal Structure (§2)

Why ordinary spectra fail

The sphere sequence $S = (S^0, S^1, S^2, \ldots)$ is a monoid in the category of sequences $\mathcal{S}^{\mathbb{N}}_*$, but not a commutative monoid (the twist map on $S^1 \wedge S^1$ is not the identity). Hence there is no closed symmetric monoidal smash product on $\mathrm{Sp}^{\mathbb{N}}$.

Why symmetric spectra work

The sphere symmetric spectrum $S$ is a commutative monoid in the category of symmetric sequences $\mathcal{S}^\Sigma_*$ (Lemma 4.4.1: it is the free commutative monoid on $G_1 S^1$).

Key equivalence (Proposition 2.2.1):

$$\mathrm{Sp}^\Sigma \;\simeq\; \text{left } S\text{-modules in } \mathcal{S}^\Sigma_*$$

Smash product (Definition 2.2.3):

$$X \wedge Y := X \otimes_S Y$$

i.e., the relative tensor product over $S$ in the symmetric monoidal category of symmetric sequences.

Main structural theorem (Theorem 2.2.10):

The smash product is a closed symmetric monoidal product on $\mathrm{Sp}^\Sigma$. In particular, there is a natural adjunction isomorphism

$$\mathrm{Sp}^\Sigma(X \wedge Y, Z) \cong \mathrm{Sp}^\Sigma(X, \mathrm{Hom}_S(Y, Z)).$$

The internal hom $\mathrm{Hom}_S(X,Y)$ is the function spectrum.

4. The Stable Category of Symmetric Spectra of Simplicial Sets

4.1 Stable Equivalence — the critical subtlety (§3.1)

⚠️ Warning: Stable equivalence cannot be defined via stable homotopy groups $\pi_*$ (i.e., via the underlying forgetful functor $U: \mathrm{Sp}^\Sigma \to \mathrm{Sp}^{\mathbb{N}}$). The forgetful functor does not preserve stable equivalences.

Definition (3.1.3): A map $f: X \to Y$ is a stable equivalence if $E^0 f: E^0 Y \to E^0 X$ is an isomorphism for every injective $\Omega$-spectrum $E$, where $E^0(X) = \pi_0 \mathrm{Map}(X, E)$.

Equivalently (Proposition 3.1.4), the following are equivalent:

  • $E^0 f$ is an isomorphism for every injective $\Omega$-spectrum $E$
  • $\mathrm{Map}(f, E)$ is a weak equivalence for every injective $\Omega$-spectrum $E$
  • $\mathrm{Hom}_S(f, E)$ is a level equivalence for every injective $\Omega$-spectrum $E$

Important relationship with homotopy groups (Theorem 3.1.11):
If $\pi_* f$ is an isomorphism, then $f$ is a stable equivalence. The converse fails in general (Example 3.1.10: the map $\lambda: F_1 S^1 \to F_0 S^0$ is a stable equivalence but not a $\pi_*$-isomorphism).

Corollary 3.1.8: Every level equivalence is a stable equivalence.

4.2 The Stable Model Structure (§3.4, Theorem 3.4.4)

Main Theorem:

$\mathrm{Sp}^\Sigma$ with the following three classes is a model category:

  • Weak equivalences = stable equivalences
  • Cofibrations = stable cofibrations (= $FI_\partial$-cofibrations = maps with LLP w.r.t. all level trivial fibrations)
  • Fibrations = stable fibrations (= maps with RLP w.r.t. all stable trivial cofibrations)

Key identifications:

  • Stable trivial fibrations = level trivial fibrations (Lemma 3.4.5)
  • Stable fibrations = $J$-injective maps (Corollary 3.4.16)
  • Fibrant objects = $\Omega$-spectra (Corollary 3.4.13), i.e., $X$ such that each $X_n$ is a Kan complex and $X_n \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathrm{Map}(S^1, X_{n+1})$
  • Cofibrant objects: $X$ is stably cofibrant iff $* \to X$ is a stable cofibration; all $F_n K$ are stably cofibrant (Prop. 3.4.2.3)

Characterization of stable cofibrations (Proposition 5.2.2):
$f: X \to Y$ is a stable cofibration iff for all $n \geq 0$, the map

$$\mathrm{Ev}_n(f \square i): X_n \amalg_{L_n X} L_n Y \to Y_n$$

is a $\Sigma_n$-cofibration (i.e., equivariant monomorphism with free $\Sigma_n$-action on new cells), where $L_n X = \mathrm{Ev}_n(X \wedge \bar{S})$ is the latching space.

4.3 Comparison with Bousfield–Friedlander (§4, Theorem 4.3.2)

The forgetful functor $U: \mathrm{Sp}^\Sigma \to \mathrm{Sp}^{\mathbb{N}}$ and its left adjoint $V: \mathrm{Sp}^{\mathbb{N}} \to \mathrm{Sp}^\Sigma$ form a Quillen equivalence of stable model categories.

Concretely: $V(X) = S \otimes_{T(G_1 S^1)} GX$. The induced equivalence on homotopy categories identifies $\mathrm{Ho}(\mathrm{Sp}^\Sigma)$ with Boardman's stable homotopy category.

The homotopy category of symmetric $\Omega$-spectra $\mathrm{Ho}(\Omega \mathrm{Sp}^\Sigma)$ (inverting level equivalences) is also naturally equivalent to Boardman's category (§1.4).

5. Additional Key Properties (§5)

5.1 Two Level Model Structures (Theorem 5.1.2)

There are two model structures with weak equivalences = level equivalences:

  • Projective level structure: cofibrations = stable cofibrations (= $FI_\partial$-cof), fibrations = level fibrations
  • Injective level structure: cofibrations = level cofibrations (= monomorphisms), fibrations = injective fibrations

Corollary 5.1.3: Every symmetric spectrum embeds in an injective spectrum by a level equivalence (enough injectives).

5.2 Monoidal Structure is Compatible (§5.3, Corollary 5.3.8)

Both the projective level structure and the stable model structure on $\mathrm{Sp}^\Sigma$ are monoidal: the pushout smash product $f \square g$ of two cofibrations is a cofibration, trivial if either factor is trivial.

Key result (Theorem 5.3.7): if $f$ is an $S$-cofibration and $g$ is a level cofibration, and either is a stable equivalence, then $f \square g$ is a stable equivalence.

Since the sphere spectrum $S$ is cofibrant in $\mathrm{Sp}^\Sigma$, this ensures the smash product descends to a symmetric monoidal structure on $\mathrm{Ho}(\mathrm{Sp}^\Sigma)$.

5.3 Proper Model Category (Theorem 5.4.2)

The stable model category of symmetric spectra is proper (both left and right proper).

5.4 The Monoid Axiom (Theorem 5.5.1)

Transfinite compositions of pushouts of maps of the form $f \wedge X$ (where $f$ is a stable trivial cofibration) are stable equivalences.

Consequence (Corollaries 5.5.2–5.5.3): For any monoid $R$ in $\mathrm{Sp}^\Sigma$ (= symmetric ring spectrum):

  • There is a model structure on $R$-modules (weak equivalences = underlying stable equivalences)
  • If $R$ is commutative, there is a model structure on $R$-algebras
  • A stable equivalence of monoids $R \xrightarrow{\sim} R'$ induces a Quillen equivalence between module/algebra categories (Theorem 5.5.9)

5.5 Semistable Spectra (§5.6)

A symmetric spectrum $X$ is semistable if $X \to LX$ (fibrant replacement) is a stable homotopy equivalence. Semistable spectra include:

  • All $\Omega$-spectra (fibrant objects)
  • Spectra with finite classical stable homotopy groups
  • Connective spectra satisfying a connectivity growth condition

Proposition 5.6.5: For semistable $X$ and $Y$, a map $f: X \to Y$ is a stable equivalence iff it is a stable homotopy equivalence.

6. The Stable Category of Topological Symmetric Spectra (§6)

6.1 Setup

One works with compactly generated weak Hausdorff spaces $\mathcal{T}_*$ (not all of $\mathrm{Top}_*$, which is not symmetric monoidal under $\wedge$). The category $\mathcal{T}_*$ is a proper monoidal model category.

A topological symmetric spectrum (Definition 6.2.2) is defined exactly as in the simplicial case, replacing simplicial sets by spaces in $\mathcal{T}_*$ and $S^1$ by the unit circle.

Structural equivalence: The category $\mathrm{Sp}^\Sigma_{\mathcal{T}_*}$ is again equivalent to left $S$-modules (where $S = (S^0, S^1, \ldots)$ with permutation actions), and is a closed symmetric monoidal category.

6.2 Stable Model Structure on Topological Symmetric Spectra (§6.3)

The stable model structure is lifted from the simplicial case via:

  • geometric realization $|\cdot|: \mathrm{Sp}^\Sigma \to \mathrm{Sp}^\Sigma_{\mathcal{T}_*}$
  • singular functor $\mathrm{Sing}: \mathrm{Sp}^\Sigma_{\mathcal{T}_*} \to \mathrm{Sp}^\Sigma$

Definition (6.3.2): A map $f$ in $\mathrm{Sp}^\Sigma_{\mathcal{T}_*}$ is:

  • a stable equivalence if $\mathrm{Sing}(f)$ is a stable equivalence in $\mathrm{Sp}^\Sigma$
  • a stable fibration if $\mathrm{Sing}(f)$ is a stable fibration in $\mathrm{Sp}^\Sigma$
  • a stable cofibration if $f$ has LLP w.r.t. all stable trivial fibrations

Theorem 6.3.8: The stable model structure on $\mathrm{Sp}^\Sigma_{\mathcal{T}_*}$ is a model category. Moreover:

  • $f \in \mathrm{Sp}^\Sigma$ is a stable equivalence $\Leftrightarrow$ $|f|$ is a stable equivalence
  • $g \in \mathrm{Sp}^\Sigma_{\mathcal{T}_*}$ is a stable equivalence $\Leftrightarrow$ $\mathrm{Sing}(g)$ is a stable equivalence
  • (Quillen equivalence): $(|\cdot|, \mathrm{Sing})$ is a Quillen equivalence between the stable model structure on $\mathrm{Sp}^\Sigma$ and the stable model structure on $\mathrm{Sp}^\Sigma_{\mathcal{T}_*}$

Fibrant objects = topological $\Omega$-spectra: $X$ such that $X_n \xrightarrow{\sim} X_{n+1}^{S^1}$ for all $n$ (Lemma 6.3.3).

6.3 Properties of the Topological Stable Structure (§6.4)

Monoidal (Corollary 6.4.2): Both the projective level structure and the stable model structure on $\mathrm{Sp}^\Sigma_{\mathcal{T}_*}$ are monoidal.

Proper (Theorem 6.4.4): The stable model structure on topological symmetric spectra is proper.

Monoids and modules (Theorem 6.4.5): The monoid axiom is unknown for topological symmetric spectra. However:

  • Every monoid $R$ admits a stably cofibrant replacement $R' \xrightarrow{\sim} R$
  • If $R$ is stably cofibrant, there is a model structure on $R$-modules
  • Quillen equivalence holds between module categories under stably cofibrant stable equivalences of monoids

Homotopy category of monoids (Theorem 6.4.6): The homotopy category of monoids in $\mathrm{Sp}^\Sigma_{\mathcal{T}_*}$ (inverting stable equivalences) is equivalent to the homotopy category of monoids in $\mathrm{Sp}^\Sigma$.
